Whirlpool Washer Not Draining: Free Checks, Error Codes and Pump Test

By the PartSnap repair desk · Reviewed against Whirlpool support pages and service manuals · Updated November 2026

Quick answer: Most Whirlpool washers that won't drain have a clog (drain hose, pump filter or coin trap), not a dead part. Do the free checks below first. If the drain pump hums but no water moves, or it reads open on a multimeter, replace the pump. If the washer also won't spin, check the lid lock.

Safety first. Unplug the washer before you touch anything inside it. Turn off both water taps. Water left in the tub may be hot and can be several gallons: have towels and a low pan ready before you open a filter or hose.

What the error code means

CodeWhere you see itMeaning
F9 E1Front-load and top-load HELong drain: the washer took too long to empty (Whirlpool cites roughly 8 minutes). Usually a blocked pump or hose.
drnTop-load HENot draining properly. Whirlpool notes this is sometimes an installation problem.
F21 / F02Older front-load (e.g. Duet)Long drain: draining is taking longer than expected.
F5 E2Front-loadDoor lock problem. Can stop drain and spin.

Codes vary by model. Check the table in your owner's manual too.

Free checks (do these first)

  1. Reset the control. Unplug the washer for 5 minutes, plug it back in and run Drain & Spin with the tub empty.
  2. Look at the drain hose. It must not be kinked behind the washer. Don't push it too far into the standpipe or seal it with tape: follow the depth in your install guide (usually about 4–6 inches).
  3. Clean the pump filter (front-load). Open the small panel at the bottom front, drain the short hose into a pan, then unscrew the filter and clear lint, coins and hair pins.
  4. Cut the suds. Use HE detergent only, in the amount on the label. Too many suds slow draining and can trigger F9 E1.
  5. Check the house drain. If the standpipe or laundry sink backs up, the clog is in your plumbing, not the washer.

Which part is most likely

  • Drain pump (most likely): You hear a hum during the drain step but the water doesn't drop, or you hear nothing at all.
  • Lid switch / door lock (less likely): The washer also refuses to spin. On many models it won't drain or spin until it senses a locked lid or door. Listen for a click when the cycle starts.

Confirm it: drain pump multimeter test

  1. Unplug the washer. Drain the water and reach the pump (back panel on many top-loads, bottom front on front-loads).
  2. Pull the wire connector off the pump. Set your meter to ohms (Ω).
  3. Touch the probes to the two pump terminals.
ReadingWhat it means
About 14–25 Ω (≈16 Ω typical)Motor winding is good. Check the impeller and hoses for a jam.
Open / infinite (OL)Winding is broken. Replace the pump.
Near 0 ΩShorted. Replace the pump.

A good reading only proves the motor winding. A broken or jammed impeller can still stop water moving. Turn it by hand: it should spin a little, then catch.

FAQ

Can I drain a Whirlpool washer by hand?

Yes. On front-loaders, use the short drain tube behind the bottom panel. On top-loaders, lower the drain hose into a bucket below the tub level, or bail the tub.

Why does F9 E1 come back after I clean the filter?

Check the hose install and suds next. If those are fine and the pump hums, the pump is the likely cause.

Is it worth repairing?

A drain pump is often a moderate DIY job with basic tools. If the washer is old and has other faults, compare with a new unit.
